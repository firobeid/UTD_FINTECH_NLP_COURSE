# Financial NLP Course : Use Cases from the Slides

__Author__ = 'Firas A Obeid'

This is an inventory of financial applications explicitly shown in the lesson slides. It separates My course-built examples from cited papers, company examples, and slide headlines. Company names below reflect the slides; their presence does not mean I built or deployed that company's system. Where a slide gives only a headline, where I still didnt complete studying the case, the technique is marked as unspecified rather than inferred. Some of these cases classify as GenAI or agentic AI, but I refrain on using these buzzwords where we are merely utilizing an LLM beyong its general text representation/generation scope.

## Course-built examples

| Lesson(s) | Use case (plain-language definition) | How the slides use it / technique | Company or attribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 4, 6, 7–8 | **News-driven stock signal:** use a stock's news to estimate whether its price may rise or fall. | Collect headlines for an equity, combine that day's headlines, and predict a next-day price-change label (BUY / NOT BUY or SELL). The course discusses fixed-horizon and triple-barrier labels, compares models, and monitors signals in paper trading. Methods include the course language-model/LSTM classifier and other classification baselines. | **My course project.** Refinitiv Eikon is the news source; the example universe includes Dow Jones, S&P 500, and Nasdaq stocks. |
| 1, 7–8 | **Financial-text sentiment:** score financial wording as positive, negative, or other sentiment. | Use the Loughran–McDonald financial lexicon to score words and calculate net tone; use sentiment classification as a baseline, separate from the stock-price label. | **Course exercise** using the Loughran–McDonald lexicon, described in the slides as designed for 10-K reports. No adopting company is named. |
| 2, 7–8 | **Financial-news topic segmentation:** group stock news into themes so analysts can compare model performance across types of news. | Assign topics to each stock's daily concatenated headlines, then evaluate the signal classifier within each topic. Methods shown include LDA, NMF, and FastTopic, with topic-coherence assessment. | **My course analysis.** No financial company is identified as a customer or adopter. |
| 4 | **Financial headline/article generation:** train a model to produce headline-like or article-like financial text. | Generate text from a seed or continue a sequence, alongside language-model representation exercises. The slides discuss stateful and stateless training. | **My course lab.** Character-level LSTM language models; no company is named. |
| 7–8 | **Similar-news retrieval for stock classification:** find past headlines like today's and use their known labels as evidence for a new signal. | Retrieve candidate training headlines, re-rank them, and combine their label votes. **My advanced system:** FinBERT embeddings with SIF aggregation; SVC decision-function scores retrieve candidates; a string-kernel score and cross-encoder re-rank; the leading results vote on the final label. | **My work**, as I specified. No external company is named for this system. |
| 9 | **Payment categorization:** sort a transaction into a category such as fitness, dining, or transportation. | Pair a transaction description with category statements and choose the best-supported category using natural-language inference (NLI), as a zero-shot classification example. | **Course slide example:** “Monthly payment to Fitness World Gym” Pseudo Example; no company is named. |

| 9 | **Financial-news sentiment with a finance-tuned LLM:** classify a news item as positive, neutral, or negative. | The slides show fetching historical headlines for AAPL and classifying financial news; they also show adapting a Llama 2 model with a PEFT/LoRA adapter using FinGPT tooling. | **FinGPT** is the named open-source project. | 10 | **Merchant/platform risk scoring:** estimate whether a merchant record signals platform fraud. | A slide labeled Stripe's risk-assessment use case demonstrates scoring the next “yes/no” token with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and normalizing the two probabilities. | 11 | **Cryptocurrency quant trading:** test whether an LLM can make systematic trades from numerical market data while following risk limits. | A zero-shot LLM decision loop produces BUY/SELL actions, stop-losses, leverage, and position sizes; the example is BTC. Evaluation includes PnL, Sharpe ratio, drawdown, and rule adherence. | Research example; no operating company is named. Several frontier LLMs are listed as candidates. |
| 11 | **Central-bank rate forecasting:** predict whether the ECB will hike, pause, or cut rates from its communications. | Fine-tune RoBERTa to classify ECB sentences as hawkish, dovish, or neutral; aggregate the scores and pass them with macro variables to a multinomial ordered-probit model. | **European Central Bank (ECB)** communications; research example. |
| 11 | **Macroeconomic sentiment tracking:** estimate growth and inflation pressures from news before official statistics arrive. | A BIS study uses hierarchical zero-/few-shot LLM classification to score articles and separate demand- from supply-driven factors, then aggregates scores into indicators. The slides name Wall Street Journal articles via Dow Jones Factiva. | **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)** study; **Dow Jones Factiva** is the data source. |
| 11 | **Financial-document triage:** help investment professionals sort financial articles, central-bank releases, research, and email. | Fine-tune Qwen3-235B using RL/RLVR, CISPO loss, and on-policy distillation to classify relevance, label content, or find where boilerplate begins and ends. | Research example using the **Tinker** platform; no financial company is named. |
| 11 | **Corporate-disclosure signal tracking:** detect when a company shifts the metrics it emphasizes in filings or earnings calls. | A zero-shot decoder extracts context-rich metrics; an encoder compares their embeddings across periods. The paper proposes using metric shifts to identify firms to short or underweight. | Research example; no company is named as the system's operator. |
| 11 | **LLM investment-bias measurement:** test whether an LLM favors certain sectors or stocks despite balanced evidence. | Prompt models with balanced buy/sell arguments for S&P 500 stocks, strengthen counter-evidence, and measure recommendation persistence and bias. The slides propose a sector-specific bias leaderboard for model selection. | Research example; the stock sample is S&P 500 companies, not named adopters. |
| 11 | **Look-ahead-bias control in stock prediction:** reduce the risk that a model recognizes a company or event from information learned after the prediction date. | “Entity neutering” removes company and sector identifiers and paraphrases text, then compares sentiment and next-day return predictability. | Research example; no company is named. |
| 11 | **Investor-disagreement measurement:** compare how different investor profiles react to the same company headline. | Prompt 216 personas with RavenPack headlines and ask for BUY/HOLD/SELL using Llama 3.1 8B; aggregate disagreement across the personas. | Research example. **FINRA** demographic survey and **RavenPack** headlines are named sources. |
| 11 | **Adversarial headline testing for trading:** test whether manipulated news can mislead an LLM-driven trading strategy. | The slide shows visually similar Cyrillic characters substituted into a company name and hidden HTML text added to headlines. | Research example; **Alphabet** is the named company in the homoglyph example. |
| 11 | **News-timing strategy:** study when market-moving information is already reflected in price. | The slide provides the paper headline “Buy the Rumor, Sell the News: When Is News Priced In?” but gives no technique or company details. | Research headline only; no company is named on the slide. |
| 11 | **Earnings-sentiment portfolio construction:** tilt a Japanese equity portfolio using earnings-news sentiment. | First adjust sector weights using market-cap-weighted sentiment, then tilt stocks within sectors; the slide gives portfolio concentration limits. | Research example; no company or asset manager is named. |
| 11 | **Visual investor-sentiment index:** use financial GIFs to measure investor mood and relate it to stock returns. | The paper creates a daily GIF-sentiment index; the slide says it forecasts returns up to a month ahead. | 11 | **Geopolitical-risk monitoring:** track how geopolitical pressure enters corporate decisions. | LLMs read earnings calls and analyst reports to identify who is applying pressure, who is targeted, the instruments used, and company responses. | Research example; no company is named. |
